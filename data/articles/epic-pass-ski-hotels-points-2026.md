The Epic Pass grew up. For 2026–27 it covers more than 90 resorts worldwide, and the interesting change is not a new flagship mountain. It is a price cut for skiers aged 13 to 30, a wider map in Austria, and a European partner system that no longer makes skiers in France book a specific hotel to unlock their days. The hotel points game around those resorts changed just as much: Hyatt and Wyndham both repriced in 2026, Marriott, Hilton and IHG price dynamically, and the best Epic redemption in North America is a Hyatt at the base of Vail Mountain.

This guide covers six programs: **World of Hyatt, Marriott Bonvoy, Hilton Honors, IHG One Rewards, Choice Privileges and Wyndham Rewards**. No other loyalty programs, and no independent lodges, however good they are. Where none of the six works, and Epic has a few famous dead zones, the guide says so plainly and names the nearest chain base instead.

> **The Loyalist angle:** A points price tells you what a night costs. It does not tell you what your status gets you at 7 a.m. on a powder day: breakfast before first lift, a waived resort fee, free parking, a late checkout that matches the last chair, or a suite upgrade that actually clears. Throughout this guide we flag the status perk that matters *for skiing*, not just in general. Been on one of these trips? Report your stay on Loyalist Travel: what you booked, what you got, and whether status moved the needle is exactly the data this guide is built to collect.

### How to Read the Points Ranges

- **Standard rooms only**, one night, winter season, unless a property is priced per bedroom (Wyndham vacation clubs and Vacasa, flagged every time).
- Ranges are **indicative for 2026–27, researched October 2026, not quotes**. Dynamic programs (Marriott, Hilton, IHG, Choice) move with cash prices daily. Refundable bookings can be repriced as prices move.
- Cash ranges, where given, are typical mid-winter ranges before taxes and fees, meant for value math, not a promise for Christmas week or Presidents' Weekend, when everything in this guide can double.

| Change | What it means in 2026 |
|---|---|

## The Slope-Side Shortlist: Best in Each Program

If you read nothing else, read this. These are the Epic Pass stays where points still beat cash by a mile in 2026:

- **Hyatt, Grand Hyatt Vail (CO).** Category 7, so 25,000 to 55,000 points under the May 2026 chart, at the base of Vail Mountain in Cascade Village with its own lift outside the door. Cash in season runs several times the points value, the resort fee disappears on award nights, and Globalist breakfast lands before first lift. The single best Epic redemption in this guide.
- **Marriott, Delta Hotels Whistler Village Suites (BC).** The value surprise in Whistler: standard rooms on many dates from around 40,000 points, suites bookable at standard rates, and a 35k certificate plus 15k top-up can reach it. The Westin up the street is the luxury pick at roughly 60,000 on peak dates; the Delta is the math pick.
- **Hilton, Waldorf Astoria Park City (UT).** Standard rooms around 110,000 points in ski season at Canyons Village, with the Frostwood Gondola across the street. The natural home for an Aspire certificate plus 5th-night-free combination.
- **IHG, the honest answer.** IHG has no slope-side flagship on the Epic map. Its strength is the town base: Holiday Inn Express properties in Breckenridge, Park City (Kimball Junction), Gunnison (for Crested Butte) and the East's chain towns, where the 4th-night-free card benefit turns $250–$350 cash nights into reasonable points math.
- **Choice, the Summit County and Gunnison play.** Comfort and Quality properties in Silverthorne and Gunnison price on date-based ranges that stay calm when dynamic programs spike. Nobody brags about these stays; everybody's points balance survives them.
- **Wyndham, Club Wyndham Park City (UT).** Fixed pricing per bedroom in Canyons Village, next door to the Waldorf. At the mid tier, a one-bedroom condo by the gondola costs a fraction of what the dynamic programs charge on the same street, and the kitchen pays for itself by day two.

## The Partner Resorts: Telluride, Sun Valley, Snowbasin

Full Epic Pass holders get 7 days at each of these. Epic Local does not include Telluride, and partner days are a full-pass benefit across the board. The lodging picture at all three is thin, each in its own way.

![Telluride seen from the ski hill](/images/articles/epic-pass-ski-hotels-points-2026/telluride.jpg "epic-2026-telluride.jpg")
*Telluride from the ski hill: seven Epic days, zero points hotels. Photo: John Fowler, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons.*

### Telluride, Colorado: No Points Hotel

**No hotel from any of the six programs exists in Telluride or Mountain Village.** The slope-side luxury set (Madeline, the Fairmont Heritage Place, Lumière, The Peaks) sits outside the six programs entirely. The nearest chain bases are Ouray (Quality Inn, Choice, roughly 50 minutes in winter) and Montrose (a full midscale chain town, 65–75 minutes). Telluride on Epic is a cash-lodging trip; the pass saves money at the ticket window, not at the hotel desk.

### Sun Valley, Idaho: One Hotel and a Fallback

| Hotel | Program | Access | 2026 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sun Valley, a Tribute Portfolio Hotel** | Marriott | Downtown Ketchum, ~5 min to the River Run base of Bald Mountain | Dynamic. The Wood River Valley's only full-service hotel in the six programs (it trades locally on the Hotel Ketchum identity) |
| AmericInn by Wyndham, Hailey | Wyndham | Hailey, ~12 miles / ~20 min south of Ketchum | Fixed chart per night |

The Sun Valley Lodge and the Limelight Ketchum sit outside the six programs. There is no Hyatt, Hilton, IHG or Choice property in the valley.

### Snowbasin, Utah: The Ogden Strategy

There is no six-program hotel at the mountain; Huntsville and Eden are independent-lodge country. The play is **Ogden, about 20–25 minutes away**, where cash prices are a fraction of Park City's and every program has a flag:

| Hampton Inn & Suites / Home2 / Tru, Ogden | Hilton | Ogden corridor | Dynamic |

Layton, about 30 minutes out, adds a second full chain cluster. Ogden is an inexpensive cash market in its own right, which is the real story: this is the one Epic partner where paying cash and saving the points is often the correct answer.

## Resorts of the Canadian Rockies: The Partner Tier

Full Epic Pass holders get **7 days total across all six RCR resorts**, no blackout dates, with half-price lift tickets after the seven are used. Two mechanical notes: RCR is **not** on the Epic Local Pass, and RCR resorts require the **physical** Epic Pass card plus photo ID; the mobile pass does not work there.

### Fernie, British Columbia

The slope-side lodges are independent, the in-town chain flagship flies a flag outside the six programs, and Fernie's old Wyndham-branded motel on Highway 3 now trades as an independent. **Verdict: we found no six-program hotel in Fernie.** The nearest chain base is Cranbrook (see Kimberley), roughly an hour and a quarter away. Fernie on Epic is a cash-lodging trip.

### Kicking Horse, British Columbia: The Golden Base

The resort village's lodges are independent. Golden, about 15 minutes down the road, carries a small and unusually Wyndham-heavy row:

| Hotel | Program | Access | 2026 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holiday Inn Express Golden–Kicking Horse | IHG | Golden, ~8.4 mi from the resort | Dynamic |
| Super 8 / Ramada Limited / Days Inn / Travelodge, Golden | Wyndham | In town | Fixed chart per night; four Wyndham brands in one small town is a genuine oddity, and a useful one |

No Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt or Choice property stands in Golden.

### Kimberley, British Columbia

Slope-side **Trickle Creek Lodge** is resort-operated and sits outside the six programs, and no in-town Kimberley hotel flies one of the six flags either. The base is **Cranbrook** (about 30 minutes): **Days Inn and Super 8** (Wyndham) and **Econo Lodge** (Choice).

### Nakiska, Alberta

| Hotel | Program | Access | 2026 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pomeroy Kananaskis Mountain Lodge** (Autograph Collection) | Marriott | Kananaskis Village, under 5 km from Nakiska; the lodge puts the chairlift at under 10 minutes | Dynamic. 317 rooms, the RCR tier's one true resort hotel in the six programs, with a renovated 71-room Black Diamond Club wing opened in May 2025 |

Canmore (35 minutes) is the fallback: **Holiday Inn Canmore** (IHG), **Super 8 Canmore** (Wyndham) and WorldMark condo stock.

### Mont-Sainte-Anne, Québec

| Hotel | Program | Access | 2026 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Delta Hotels Mont Sainte-Anne** | Marriott | Beaupré, **at the foot of the mountain, ski-in/ski-out**, billed as the only resort there with direct slope access | Dynamic. About 30 minutes from Québec City |

### Stoneham, Québec

The Hôtel Stoneham is resort-run and independent. The base is **Québec City** (about 30 minutes): DoubleTree and Hampton Inn & Suites Beauport (Hilton), Delta Québec, Hotel PUR and Courtyard (Marriott), Travelodge and Super 8 (Wyndham), and Choice's Ascend and Quality flags. IHG's option is the Holiday Inn Express Québec City Sainte-Foy; we found no Hyatt in the city.

## Europe: The Partner Map

Epic's European access runs on partner mechanics, and the details matter more than the hotel list. Days are exchanged at a ticket window with the physical pass and photo ID; the mobile pass does not scan at partner resorts. Several allotments are **consecutive days**, and the Epic Day Pass includes neither Verbier nor Japan.

| Area | Epic access (full pass) | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| Les 3 Vallées, France | 7 consecutive days | The old partner-hotel lodging requirement is gone for 2026–27 |
| Skirama Dolomiti, Italy | 7 consecutive days | Eight areas under one allotment, Madonna di Campiglio the headline |
| Verbier 4 Vallées, Switzerland | 5 consecutive days | Also the only European partner on Epic Local, with restricted dates |

### Skirama Dolomiti: Madonna di Campiglio and Pinzolo

No core-brand hotel from the six programs stands in Madonna di Campiglio itself. The points route is **Lefay Resort & SPA Dolomiti in Pinzolo**, a few minutes down the valley, which carries Small Luxury Hotels of the World membership and with it the Hilton Honors route into the Skirama area, at dynamic prices. Beyond that, Campiglio is independent-hotel country, and the honest recommendation mirrors Crested Butte: the pass covers the skiing, cash covers the bed.

### Crans-Montana and Andermatt: Vail's Own Swiss Resorts

Both resorts are unlimited on the full Epic Pass, which makes their lodging gaps more noticeable.

- **Crans-Montana:** **Six Senses Crans-Montana** is ski-in/ski-out and flies an IHG brand flag; its points bookability has been inconsistent since its 2023 opening, so it reads as a bonus when it prices on points rather than a plan to build a trip around. The rest of the town's luxury set sits outside the six programs.
- **Andermatt:** a genuine dead zone for the six. The Radisson Blu in Andermatt belongs to Radisson's European program, and Choice Privileges' Radisson connection covers the Americas only, so it is not a Choice redemption. The Chedi and the village hotels sit outside the six as well. Andermatt on Epic is a cash stay, ideally a short one paired with the unlimited skiing.

## Australia: Perisher, Falls Creek, Hotham

All three are Vail-owned and sit on the pass for the southern winter (June to October). The lodging answer is the shortest in this guide: **none of the six programs has a hotel in Jindabyne, Perisher, Bright, Hotham or Falls Creek.** The headline chain properties in those towns (Rydges at Jindabyne, QT at Falls Creek) run their own house programs. Australian Epic skiing on points means earning the points somewhere else; the beds are cash, and booking early matters more than any loyalty angle.

## How to Stock Up: 2026 Edition

| Program | Best move in 2026 | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Hyatt | Bilt (1:1) and Chase, with the Sapphire Preferred / Ink Preferred ratio now at **4:3 since Oct 1, 2026**; Sapphire Reserve holds 1:1 | Category 1–7 certificates no longer reach Category 8 ski icons (Park Hyatt Beaver Creek) at any band |
| Marriott | Earn through stays and cards; transfer ratios from Amex and Chase are usually poor value against cash | Resort fees on award nights at the big ski properties, and dynamic prices that double between a Tuesday and a powder Saturday |
| Hilton | **Amex 1:2** is the engine; frequent transfer bonuses stretch it further | Premium-room pricing dressed up as availability at the HGV properties; the standard-room rate is the one that counts |
| IHG | Chase and Bilt transfers for a specific stay; buy-points sales near 0.5¢ are often rational where cash runs $250+ | No slope-side flagship on the Epic map; IHG value here is a town-base strategy, and the 4th night free does most of the work |
| Choice | **Wells Fargo 1:2** remains the standout transfer in US loyalty; Citi dropped to 1:1.5 in April 2026 | Date-based pricing crept more dynamic in 2026; the calm prices live in base towns (Silverthorne, Gunnison) rather than resort villages |
| Wyndham | Capital One and other bank transfers at modest ratios; co-branded Earner cards for the redemption discount | The **Sept 15, 2026 reshuffle moved individual hotels between tiers**; a hotel that was 15k last season may be 30k (or 45k) now, so the specific property's current tier is what matters |

**The universal ski-trip rules, 2026 edition:**

1. **Book points the day the pass goes on sale.** Slope-side award inventory (Grand Hyatt Vail, Park Hyatt Beaver Creek, the Delta in Whistler) is a spring game, not a December game.
2. **Use the multiplier, not the headline.** A 60k Marriott night is 48k effective across a 5-night stay. A 50k IHG night is 37.5k effective across a 4-night cardholder stay. Effective prices are the honest comparison.
3. **Reprice dynamic awards as the season moves.** Marriott, Hilton, IHG and Choice prices fall when cash falls. A refundable award booking costs nothing to rebook lower.
4. **Match the program to the resort's shape.** Hyatt owns Vail and Beaver Creek's best math; Marriott owns Whistler and the partner-resort anchors (Ketchum, Hakuba, Rusutsu, Verbier); Hilton owns Breckenridge's new base hotel and Park City's certificate play; Choice and Wyndham own the base towns; IHG owns the 4th night in those same towns.
5. **Respect the dead zones.** Telluride, Crested Butte's mountain village, Andermatt and the Australian resorts have no six-program answer. A guide that pretends otherwise is selling something; this one would rather save the points for a resort where they work.

## Bottom Line

The Epic Pass in 2026 is a Marriott-and-Hyatt story with a long tail. Hyatt holds the two best redemptions on the map (Grand Hyatt Vail at Category 7, Park Hyatt Beaver Creek at Category 8) and the best budget one (Hyatt Place Keystone). Marriott holds the widest slope-side footprint, from Whistler's Village to the W in Verbier to the only points hotels in Ketchum, Hakuba and Rusutsu. Hilton holds Park City's certificate play and Breckenridge's new base-area flagship. IHG, Choice and Wyndham hold the base towns, Gunnison, Silverthorne, Ogden and Kimball Junction, where most Epic trips actually sleep on points.

And the pass itself changed the trip math for younger skiers: a 13-to-30 pass at 20% off buys a lot of hotel nights' worth of savings before the first lift ride. The resorts where points do not work (Telluride, Andermatt, Perisher) are named plainly above, because the fastest way to waste a points balance is forcing it into a town that never took it.

*Skiing one of these this winter? [Report your stay on Loyalist Travel](/submit-a-stay): the status held, the room booked, the room received, and what breakfast, parking and late checkout actually looked like. The 2027 edition of this guide should be built on verified stays, not award calendars.*
